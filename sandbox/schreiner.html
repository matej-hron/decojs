Schreiner Equation

Linear-rate companion to the Haldane page — watch the equation evolve as ambient pressure slides.

Two targets, two equations

Haldane — fixed target

  • At t = 0, alveolar pressure is set to a new value.
  • It then remains constant.
  • The tissue approaches it exponentially.

Schreiner — moving target

  • Alveolar pressure rises or falls linearly during a depth change.
  • The tissue continuously responds to the current pressure difference.
  • The diver's direction alone does not determine whether the tissue is on-gassing or off-gassing.

Situation: the equilibrium target moves

During descent or ascent, alveolar pressure changes linearly while tissue pressure responds progressively according to the compartment half-time.

Start with the tissue's initial pressure. In the basic equilibrium example the initial Haldane contribution is zero, so only the response to the moving alveolar target is added.

2. Reaction to the moving target

R · [t − (1 − e−kt)/k]

Alveolar pressure does not remain at its initial value. This term adds the effect of its continuous linear movement.

Imagine the moving target as many small pressure changes. The tissue responds to each one in the same exponential way as in Haldane's equation; their sum forms this correction.

General case: the tissue may start away from equilibrium

In a complete dive profile, tissue pressure at the start of a new segment need not equal current alveolar pressure. The general teaching form therefore also contains the Haldane contribution from the initial pressure difference.

pt(t) = pt,0 + (palv,0 − pt,0) · (1 − e−kt) + R · [t − (1 − e−kt)/k]

How the teaching form becomes the canonical equation

Expand the two brackets, cancel equal opposite terms, and group the remaining exponential terms.

initial state Haldane contribution moving-target correction

Teaching form

pt(t) = pt,0 + (palv,0 − pt,0) · (1 − e−kt) + R · [t − (1 − e−kt)/k]

Expand the brackets

pt(t) = +pt,0 + palv,0 − pt,0 − (palv,0 − pt,0)e−kt + R·t − R/k + (R/k)e−kt

The terms +pt,0 and −pt,0 cancel.

Group the terms

pt(t) = palv,0 + R·t − R/k − (palv,0 − pt,0 − R/k)e−kt

Canonical form

This closed form calculates the exact continuous tissue response without splitting the depth change into many small time steps.

pt(t) = palv,0 + R · (t − 1/k) − (palv,0 − pt,0 − R/k) · e−kt

Why does the moving-target correction have this form?

A linear alveolar-pressure change can be divided into many very small changes. Each occurs at a different time and therefore has a different amount of time to affect the tissue. Adding those exponential responses produces the moving-target correction.

For readers familiar with calculus, both Haldane and Schreiner solve the same differential law:

dpt/dt = k · [palv(t) − pt(t)]
